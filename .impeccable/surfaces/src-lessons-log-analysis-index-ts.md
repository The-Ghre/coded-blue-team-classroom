## Direction contract

THESIS: The lesson is a dispatcher's graphic timetable (Ibry/Marey train graph). Log sources are stations, time runs left to right, ordinary activity is scheduled traffic drawn in advance as a regular lattice of diagonals; the attack is the unscheduled train — a vermilion run that waits, then plunges through stations faster than anything on the sheet. Refuses the SIEM dashboard, the decorative terminal and the cream-and-serif case file.

OWN-WORLD: Pale graph-pad green sheet, green ruling ink for the minute grid, graphite for type and scheduled traffic, signal vermilion reserved for the unscheduled run (adversary). Plex Sans Condensed caps for station names and headlines, Plex Mono tabular times, Plex Sans for reading. Rectilinear: 2px corners, hairline rules, leader-line annotations attached to the exact stop they describe. Spot-ink discipline (raised from the Riso challenger): every colour is a named ink with one job.

STORY: Students see that the baseline is knowable in advance (the timetable), the attack is only known as it runs, and a stop recorded at two stations is what makes it confirmed. They select lines, flag runs, and watch the route draw.

FIRST VIEWPORT: Present title slide: full-bleed train graph, station names stacked down the left margin, hour/minute ticks across the top, the scheduled lattice in faint graphite; "Session 2" set huge in condensed caps over the left half; the vermilion run drawing in to the now-line.
